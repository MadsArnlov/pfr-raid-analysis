Phase reached · pull composition

Each bar is one raid night, split by how far pulls that night got. As phase 1 shrinks and phase 3 grows, the group is spending less time re-clearing old ground and more time on the current wall. The line tracks the single best pull (lowest boss HP%) of the night.

Volume · duration

Pull count per night (bars) against average pull length in seconds (line). Fewer, longer pulls is the expected shape as prog gets deeper — each attempt survives further before dying.

Pitfalls — where pulls end

Wall detection by phase

Distribution of boss HP% at time of death, grouped by the phase each pull reached. A tall bar means many pulls die at almost exactly the same HP — usually a specific mechanic, not bad luck.

Pull conversion — phase to phase

Survival rate by phase transition

Of the pulls that reached a given phase, the share that survived it and pushed into the next one (or, for the deepest phase reached, into an actual kill). A low conversion rate means that phase itself is the bottleneck; a high rate means pulls are blowing through it and dying further out.

Same transitions, tracked night over night. Rising lines mean a transition is getting easier as the group learns it; a flat or falling line on an earlier transition (e.g. P1→P2) while later ones improve usually just means it's fully solved and any remaining drops are noise. Gaps mean no pulls reached that phase that night.

Progression velocity

Pulls between all-time-best records

Every time a pull beats the previous all-time-best HP% remaining, it's logged as a record. The bars show how many pulls it took to reach each new record — this naturally grows as prog gets deeper, but a sudden spike (a bar much taller than its neighbors) is a sign of a real wall rather than expected slowdown.
